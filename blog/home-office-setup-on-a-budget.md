  A home office setup on a budget starts with fixing how your screen, back, and
  hands sit before you buy anything. Most of the upgrades below cost nothing or
  under $100, and only one or two need a real purchase. We ranked seven changes
  by how much they improve daily comfort and concentration, with the cheapest
  high-impact fixes first. If you only do three things this week, do the first
  three.


  ## How we ranked the seven upgrades


  Each upgrade gets a simple test. Does it reduce the physical strain you feel
  every day? Does it remove a visual or sound interruption that breaks your
  focus? Items that do both rank highest. Price matters, but it comes second,
  because a free fix you actually use beats a gadget that sits in a box.


  ## 1. Raise Your Screen to Eye Level


  ### Why it ranks first


  Screen height affects your neck and shoulders more than almost anything else
  on the desk. OSHA's [computer workstations
  guide](https://www.osha.gov/etools/computer-workstations) recommends that the
  top of the monitor sit at or just below eye level, and that you sit at least
  20 inches (50 cm) from the screen.


  ### How to do it


  - Stack books, a sturdy box, or a storage bin under your laptop until the top
  of the screen lines up with your eyes.

  - If you want a dedicated fix, a laptop stand is the first purchase many
  budget guides recommend.

  - Keep your elbows bent at a relaxed angle and your wrists straight.


  **Budget:** $0 to under $100.

  ## 2. Add Lumbar Support


  ### The fix you feel by mid-afternoon


  Lower-back fatigue is what makes most people slump, shift, and lose their
  train of thought. OSHA's [chair
  guidance](https://www.osha.gov/etools/computer-workstations/components/chairs)
  says that if your chair has no lumbar support, a rolled-up towel or a
  removable back support cushion can serve as an interim fix.


  In our view, this upgrade ranks above a new chair because it costs almost
  nothing and changes how you sit from the first day.


  **Budget:** free (a towel) to under $100 (a cushion).


  ## 3. Upgrade Your Chair, Used First


  The chair is where most of your hours go, so it earns the third spot.
  Discover's [budget home office
  guide](https://www.discover.com/online-banking/banking-topics/home-office-on-a-budget/)
  makes a useful point: most people don't need a high-end office swivel chair to
  work comfortably.


  A new budget ergonomic chair can run around $165 to $200. The Sihoo M18 is one
  example cited in [Home Office Nomad's $500
  build](https://homeofficenomad.com/500-home-office-setup/). A used commercial
  chair can cost less. One ranking guide calls a used Steelcase or Herman Miller
  chair from a reputable dealer the single most important item in a home office.


  ### What to check on a used chair


  - **Gas cylinder:** Does the seat hold its height, or does it slowly sink? A
  failing cylinder makes a chair unpleasant to use every day.

  - **Welds and frame:** Look for cracks at the joints and under the seat.

  - **Stress points:** Check seams, armrest bases, and the front edge of the
  seat for tears.

  - **Adjustments:** Confirm that seat height, armrests, and lumbar support all
  still move.


  **Budget:** around $165 to $200 for a new budget chair, or less for a used
  one.


  ## 4. Add an External Keyboard and Mouse


  When you work on a laptop, the keyboard sits at the same height as the screen,
  so the screen ends up too low. The fix is to separate the input devices.
  Ireland's Health and Safety Authority says a laptop should be connected to a
  separate monitor and keyboard for regular work, as described in its [display
  screen equipment
  FAQ](https://www.hsa.ie/eng/workplace_health/manual_handling_display_screen_equipment/faqs/display_screen_equipment_faqs/display_screen_equipment1/).


  A separate keyboard and mouse let you raise the screen and keep your wrists in
  a neutral position. If you want a shortlist, our [budget mechanical keyboards
  roundup](https://www.3minread.com/best-budget-mechanical-keyboards) compares
  six picks.


  **Budget:** under $100 for a wireless set.

  ## 5. Add a Second Monitor


  A second screen reduces the window juggling that scatters attention. You can
  keep reference material, a calendar, or a video call open without switching
  tabs every few seconds. Place it at the same distance and height as your main
  screen so you aren't turning your head all day.


  Used monitors are the cheapest route. Home Office Nomad notes that a used Dell
  P2419H sells for roughly $60 to $90, and the Sihoo M18 chair appears in the
  same guide. Used prices shift, so check current listings and the seller's
  return policy before you buy.


  **Budget:** about $60 to $90 used.


  ## 6. Get a Desk Surface That Fits Your Height


  A desk that is too low or too high is the quiet problem behind many aches.
  Several budget builds use IKEA's LINNMON tabletop, which typically sells for
  $30 to $50, paired with ADILS legs. A used desk or a dining table also works
  if the height suits you.


  Test the setup before you commit. With the keyboard in front of you, your
  elbows should bend at a comfortable angle and your shoulders should stay
  relaxed. If you have to lift your shoulders to type, the desk is too high.


  **Budget:** about $30 to $50 for a tabletop, plus legs.


  ## 7. Cut Glare, Noise, and Clutter


  This upgrade is about focus rather than posture. Focus gains are harder to
  measure than posture gains, so treat these as practical fixes rather than
  guaranteed results.


  - **Glare:** Don't place the screen directly facing a window. If sunlight hits
  the display, close the blinds or move the desk.

  - **Light:** Use a task lamp you can aim at the desk, so the light falls on
  your papers and keyboard instead of bouncing off the screen.

  - **Noise:** A rug, a bookshelf, or a rolled towel along the bottom of a door
  can soften echo and block some household sound.

  - **Cables:** Velcro ties or a cable tray keep cords off the floor, so the
  desk looks ordered and you spend less time untangling it.


  **Budget:** often free, or low cost for a lamp.

  ## What to Skip for Now


  Honest guidance matters as much as a shopping list. Skip these until the
  basics are in place:


  - **Premium gaming or executive chairs.** A well-adjusted used chair often
  beats an expensive one that doesn't fit you.

  - **A full standing desk.** Discover makes a helpful point that you don't have
  to buy an official standing desk to get the benefit of changing positions. A
  stack of books or a simple converter is a start.

  - **A second monitor before your height and back support are sorted.** Fix the
  basics first.

  - **Decorative extras.** Plants and wall art make a room pleasant, but they
  don't change comfort or focus.

  ## Take Breaks, Not Just Better Gear


  Equipment helps, but movement matters too. The Health and Safety Authority
  notes that no single continuous period at a screen should, in general, exceed
  one hour. Set a timer, stand up, refill your water, and look at something far
  away for a moment. If you struggle to stop on time, our guide to [setting
  boundaries when working from
  home](https://www.3minread.com/how-to-set-boundaries-when-working-from-home)
  covers routines that stick.


  ## Frequently Asked Questions


  ### What should I buy first if I only have $100?


  Start with the free fixes: raise your screen and add a rolled towel for
  lower-back support. Then spend the money on a wireless keyboard and mouse if
  you use a laptop, or on a lumbar cushion if your chair has no support.


  ### What is the minimum I should spend on a home office?


  There is no fixed minimum. Some budget builds reach a working, comfortable
  setup around $500, especially when they include a used ergonomic chair. Begin
  with free changes and add items as your budget allows.


  ### Is a used office chair safe to buy?


  It can be, if you check the gas cylinder, the welds, and the stress points
  before you pay. Test the chair in person when you can, and avoid any chair
  that sinks or wobbles.


  ### Can I use a laptop as my main computer?


  Yes, with conditions. Connect it to a separate monitor and keyboard, raise the
  screen, and take regular breaks. The one-hour guidance in the break section
  applies here too.


  ### Do I need a standing desk?


  No. Changing positions matters more than the desk itself, so a simple
  converter or a stack of books can serve the same purpose for less money.


  ## The Takeaway


  Start with screen height and back support, then add a keyboard, a chair, and a
  monitor in that order as your budget allows. Fix light and noise last, but
  don't skip them. A home office setup on a budget works best when every
  purchase solves a problem you feel every day.
