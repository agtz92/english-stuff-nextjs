  To clean a cast iron skillet without ruining the seasoning, wipe out food
  while the pan is still warm, wash it with hot water and a brush, dry it right
  away, heat it briefly on the stove, and finish with a thin wipe of oil. Most
  seasoning damage comes from a handful of habits: soaking, dishwashers, harsh
  scrubbing, and sudden temperature changes. Below, you'll find the routine, a
  clear rule on soap, a list of habits to drop, and a fix if you've already
  stripped the surface.


  ## How to clean a cast iron skillet: the quick routine


  This routine takes about five minutes and works for most everyday messes.


  1. **Clean it while it's still warm.** Let the pan cool until it's safe to
  handle, then scrape out loose food with a wooden spatula or plastic scraper.
  Residue loosens more easily when the iron is warm.

  2. **Wash with hot water.** Use a sponge or a nylon brush and hot water. Skip
  anything abrasive for routine cleaning.

  3. **Dry it immediately.** Dry the skillet with a clean towel or paper towel
  as soon as you rinse it. The University of Kentucky Extension advises drying
  right away to prevent rust, and standing water is where rust starts.

  4. **Heat it dry.** Set the pan back on the burner for a minute or two until
  every trace of moisture has evaporated. [Tom's
  Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/how-to/how-to-clean-a-cast-iron-skillet-and-remove-rust)
  recommends this step so no water stays trapped in the surface.

  5. **Oil it lightly.** Add roughly one teaspoon of vegetable oil and spread it
  over the inside with a paper towel. Wipe until the surface looks almost dry.
  The goal is a layer so thin it's nearly invisible.


  That's the whole routine. If you do it after every use, you'll rarely need
  anything more.

  ## Does dish soap ruin cast iron seasoning?


  No, not in small amounts. A small squeeze of mild dish soap is fine on a
  well-seasoned pan. [Lodge's care
  guidance](https://www.lodgecastiron.com/pages/cleaning-and-care-cast-iron-oils-cast-iron-cooking-and-seasoning)
  says a small amount of mild dish soap won't damage a good layer of seasoning,
  and that's the rule we'd follow.


  Here's why it works. Seasoning is oil that has been heated until it bonds to
  the iron, so a quick wash with a drop of soap rarely lifts it. What does cause
  problems is repeated exposure to strong detergents, long soaks, and dishwasher
  cycles. Use soap for greasy or sticky residue, rinse well, and then dry and
  oil the pan as usual.

  ## Habits that strip cast iron seasoning


  These are the most common mistakes, and each one has a simple alternative.


  - **Putting it in the dishwasher.** Long wet cycles and harsh detergent can
  strip the surface and invite rust. [Tom's
  Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/how-to/how-to-clean-a-cast-iron-skillet-and-remove-rust)
  advises against both soaking and dishwashers for this reason. Hand-wash
  instead.

  - **Soaking overnight.** Water left in the pan reaches bare spots in the
  seasoning. If food is stuck, simmer a little water in the pan for about a
  minute and then scrub.

  - **Using steel wool or harsh scouring pads.** These are designed to remove
  material, and the seasoning goes with it. Save heavy scrubbing for rust spots
  only.

  - **Shocking a hot pan with cold water.** A sudden temperature change can warp
  or crack the iron. Let the skillet cool a bit before it gets rinsed.

  - **Using bleach soaks.** Some guides suggest soaking cast iron in diluted
  bleach. Skip it. Harsh chemicals and long soaks are a poor trade for your
  seasoning.

  - **Running it through the oven's self-clean cycle.** A Lodge Cast Iron
  representative, Mark Kelly, told [Consumer
  Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/frying-pans/how-should-you-clean-a-cast-iron-skillet)
  that "The high heat melts the seasoning off the pan." Its tester also called
  the approach overkill for general cleaning.

  - **Leaving acidic food in the pan.** Tomato sauce, wine reductions, and
  citrus-heavy dishes can strip young seasoning if they sit. Transfer the food
  to a plate and clean the pan soon after cooking.

  - **Air drying.** Letting the pan sit wet is the fastest route to rust. Dry it
  by hand and heat it on the stove.

  A sticky surface is usually a sign of too much oil. A thick layer doesn't
  fully bond, so it stays tacky. The fix is to wash, dry, heat, and wipe on a
  much thinner coat.


  ## Why drying and a thin oil layer matter


  Heat turns oil into a hard, slick coating. When you heat a thin layer of oil
  past its smoke point, the oil breaks down and bonds to the iron. [Lodge
  explains](https://www.lodgecastiron.com/pages/how-to-season) that the heat
  causes the oil to polymerize, bonding it to the cast iron.


  That's why the last two steps matter. A thin layer gives the oil a chance to
  cure into a smooth finish, and full drying keeps moisture from getting
  underneath it. Lodge's test kitchen often reaches for vegetable or canola oil,
  according to [Lodge's oils
  guide](https://www.lodgecastiron.com/pages/cleaning-and-care-cast-iron-oils-cast-iron-cooking-and-seasoning),
  so those are easy, dependable choices for the post-wash wipe.

  ## What to do if you've already damaged the seasoning


  If the surface looks gray, dull, or rusty, don't panic. You can usually
  restore it.


  1. **Remove the rust.** Scrub the affected area with a scouring pad, a little
  soap, or a rust eraser product.

  2. **Dry it completely.** Heat the pan on the stove until no moisture remains.

  3. **Re-season it.** Wipe on a thin layer of oil and bake the pan upside down
  in a hot oven. [Lodge's seasoning
  guide](https://www.lodgecastiron.com/pages/how-to-season) recommends
  preheating your oven to 450–500°F.

  4. **Repeat if needed.** A few thin layers usually build a better surface than
  one thick one.


  Enameled cast iron is a different case. Its glass-like coating doesn't need
  seasoning, so these rules apply to bare iron only.


  ## Frequently asked questions


  ### Can you put cast iron in the dishwasher?


  No. Dishwasher cycles combine harsh detergent with long wet exposure, which
  can strip seasoning and cause rust. Hand-wash your skillet instead.


  ### Is it okay to use dish soap on cast iron?


  Yes, a small amount of mild dish soap is fine on a well-seasoned pan. Avoid
  strong detergents and long soaks.


  ### Can I soak cast iron overnight?


  Avoid it. Soaking lets water reach bare iron and encourages rust. For stuck-on
  food, simmer a little water for about a minute instead.


  ### Should I let cast iron air dry?


  No. Dry it with a towel right away, then heat it on the stove until the last
  moisture is gone.


  ## The bottom line


  Clean your skillet while it's warm, wash it with hot water, dry it
  immediately, heat it briefly, and wipe on a thin layer of oil. Skip the
  dishwasher, long soaks, harsh abrasives, and bleach, and your seasoning will
  hold up for years. For most messes, that simple routine is all you need.
