  ## Why connection type decides the best wireless mechanical keyboard


  Every wireless mechanical keyboard reaches your computer in one of three ways:
  wired USB, a 2.4GHz USB dongle, or Bluetooth. Wired is the baseline. A
  dedicated 2.4GHz link comes closest, and [EWE ADN's latency
  comparison](https://www.eweadn.com/blogs/news/keyboard-latency-wired-vs-24ghz-vs-bluetooth)
  puts good implementations at 1 to 4 ms end to end when they poll at 1000 Hz.
  Bluetooth is slower and more variable. [Keebs for
  All](https://keebsforall.com/blogs/mechanical-keyboards-101/2-4ghz-wireless-protocols-what-you-need-to-know)
  gives a Bluetooth range of 7 to 20 ms, and other guides list higher figures,
  so treat Bluetooth as the flexible option rather than the fastest one.


  The gap starts in the standard. The Bluetooth HID-over-GATT profile allows
  connection intervals from 7.5 ms to 50 ms, as described in a [Texas
  Instruments application
  note](https://www.ti.com.cn/cn/lit/an/swra715/swra715.pdf?ts=1656944702493).
  Shorter intervals cut delay but use more power, so keyboard makers tune that
  trade-off to balance speed and battery life.

  ## Battery life: read the rating before you trust it


  Battery life is the criterion where spec sheets are most misleading. A rating
  can change with the backlight, the connection mode, and the polling rate, and
  some ratings assume conditions you will not use. Connection type,
  backlighting, and polling rate are the biggest factors. The longest runtimes
  usually come from Bluetooth with no RGB lighting.


  Before you compare boards, check four things:


  - Whether the rating is for backlight off, backlight on, or both

  - Which connection mode the rating assumes

  - Whether the rating assumes a sleep timer

  - Whether the battery is rechargeable or replaceable


  If a board lists only one number, ask the maker which setting it refers to
  before you decide.


  ## Latency: when the difference matters


  For most typing and office work, the gap between wired, 2.4GHz, and Bluetooth
  is hard to notice. It matters more in rhythm games and fast shooters, where
  timing is tight. Our view: use the 2.4GHz or wired link for gaming, and use
  Bluetooth for work and travel, where a few extra milliseconds rarely change
  the result.


  Bluetooth polling adds another layer. Sources disagree on the ceiling, with
  some reporting 125 Hz and others 250 Hz. Either way, Bluetooth is the slower
  route for games, so keep the dedicated link for matches.


  ## Multi-device pairing: what to test


  Most boards list three paired devices, but the count matters less than how
  switching works. Test these four things:


  - **Switch method:** Dedicated keys or an Fn combination should switch between
  paired devices without re-pairing.

  - **Reconnect after sleep:** A board that reconnects on its own saves time
  when you wake a laptop.

  - **OS mapping:** Check that modifier keys behave correctly on each system you
  use.

  - **Dongle plus Bluetooth:** Confirm whether you can use both at once, because
  some boards allow it and others do not.

  ## Frequently asked questions


  ### Are wireless mechanical keyboards as fast as wired ones?


  Usually they are close. Wired and good 2.4GHz links both sit in the 1 to 4 ms
  range, while Bluetooth is slower and more variable. For typing and casual
  play, the difference is rarely noticeable.


  ### Should I use Bluetooth or a 2.4GHz dongle?


  Choose Bluetooth for broad device support, such as a phone or tablet. Choose
  the 2.4GHz dongle for lower delay on a single computer. A dual-mode board
  gives you both.


  ### Can one wireless keyboard switch between devices instantly?


  Most multi-device boards use dedicated keys or Fn combinations to switch
  between paired devices without re-pairing. Speed and reconnect behavior vary,
  so test the switch on each machine you use.


  ### Does backlighting drain the battery quickly?


  Yes, backlighting is one of the biggest factors in battery life. Compare the
  backlight-on and backlight-off ratings, and plan around the setting you will
  actually use.
