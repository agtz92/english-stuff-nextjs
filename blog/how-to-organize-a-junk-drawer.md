  Want to organize a junk drawer fast? Here is the short answer: empty it
  completely, sort everything into keep, relocate, or toss piles, contain what
  stays with a few simple dividers, and follow one clear rule to stop it from
  filling up again. Done well, the whole reset takes about 30 minutes, and you
  can start without buying anything.


  A junk drawer usually fills up because it is the default spot for anything
  without a home. An HGTV professional organizer argues that most junk drawer
  problems are really 'too much stuff' problems, not organization problems
  ([HGTV](https://www.hgtv.com/lifestyle/clean-and-organize/organizing-junk-drawers)).
  That means the best fix is not a new tray. It is making fewer decisions later
  by sorting now.


  ## What You Need Before You Start


  - A trash bag and a recycling bin

  - A box or basket for items that belong in another room

  - A towel or baking sheet to empty the drawer onto

  - A tape measure

  - Optional: dividers, small bins, or containers you already own


  Measure before you buy anything. [Consumer
  Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/home-organization/quick-and-easy-decluttering-tips)
  advises measuring the drawer first for a proper fit, and it recommends
  avoiding mesh trays, which can snag paper clips and pencil tips.

  ## How to Organize a Junk Drawer in 30 Minutes


  Use this timeline. Each stage ends with a checkpoint, so you know when to move
  on.


  ### Minutes 0 to 3: Empty the drawer


  Pull the drawer out and dump everything onto your towel or tray. Resist the
  urge to put anything back yet.


  **Checkpoint:** the drawer is completely empty.


  ### Minutes 3 to 10: Sort into three piles


  Make three piles: Keep, Relocate, and Toss. [Budget
  Dumpster](https://www.budgetdumpster.com/blog/how-to-organize-a-junk-drawer-in-25-minutes)
  describes a similar split, noting that most drawers hold things that belong,
  things that don't, and trash. Be quick. If you can't decide within a few
  seconds, put the item in Keep for now and test it with the rule below.


  **Checkpoint:** every item is in a pile, and nothing is left on the tray.


  ### Minutes 10 to 15: Send relocated items home


  Walk the Relocate pile to its new home. Tools go to the garage or a toolbox,
  and stamps and envelopes go to a desk or office drawer. Put the Toss pile in a
  bag by the door so it leaves the house with you.


  **Checkpoint:** the Relocate pile is empty, and the Toss bag is ready to go.


  ### Minutes 15 to 18: Clean and measure


  Wipe the drawer interior with a damp cloth and let it dry. Measure the width,
  depth, and height of the interior, then write the numbers down on your phone.


  **Checkpoint:** you have measurements in hand before you shop or assemble
  anything.


  ### Minutes 18 to 24: Contain the keepers


  Group Keep items by how you use them, such as writing tools, tape and
  fasteners, spare batteries, and chargers. Give each group its own zone with
  dividers, small bins, or containers you already own. Clear containers help you
  see what you have, so you are less likely to buy duplicates.


  **Checkpoint:** every group has an obvious zone.


  ### Minutes 24 to 30: Reload by frequency


  Put the items you reach for most at the front and rarely used ones toward the
  back. Close the drawer, then open it once more. Does it slide shut smoothly,
  and can you find a pen in five seconds? If yes, you are done.


  **Checkpoint:** the drawer closes easily, and your most-used items are within
  reach.

  ## The One Rule That Keeps a Junk Drawer Tidy


  Most drawers get messy again because nobody has a clear test for what stays.
  Use this one: an item stays in the junk drawer only if you use it daily or
  weekly, or if it would be helpful in a pinch, such as during a power outage.
  [Budget
  Dumpster](https://www.budgetdumpster.com/blog/how-to-organize-a-junk-drawer-in-25-minutes)
  uses this same keep test.


  Everything else needs a different home. Items you use monthly often work
  better in a closet, a kitchen cabinet, or a desk. Items you use once a year,
  like holiday candles or spare wrapping paper, belong in storage.


  The rule works because it is easy to apply in the moment. When you put
  something in the drawer, ask yourself whether it passes the test. If it
  doesn't, it goes elsewhere.


  ### A Five-Minute Monthly Check


  The drawer will drift, even with a good system.
  [Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/do-i-really-need-junk-drawer.htm)
  suggests reassessing your junk drawer about once a month and reorganizing as
  needed. Set a five-minute reminder on the first of each month. Pull out
  anything that no longer passes the test, and put the rest back in its zone.

  ## Low-Cost Organizer Ideas That Work


  You don't need to spend much to get a tidy drawer. Dollar stores carry plastic
  bins in many sizes, which helps once you have your measurements. If you want
  to spend nothing, these options work well:


  - Shoe boxes or cereal boxes cut down to size as small trays

  - Egg cartons for small items such as paper clips, buttons, or thumbtacks

  - Jar lids or small cans for pens and pencils

  - Cardboard dividers made from a box you already have


  Your total cost is zero if you use what you already have. If you add a few
  plastic bins, measure first and compare sizes and prices at your store, since
  both vary by retailer. Clear containers with straight sides usually fit a
  drawer better than mesh trays, which can catch small items.

  ## Mistakes to Avoid


  - **Buying organizers before sorting.** You can't tell what size you need
  until the drawer is empty and the keepers are in a pile.

  - **Skipping the Toss pile.** If it stays on the counter, it will end up back
  in the drawer.

  - **Making one giant zone.** Small groups are easier to maintain than one
  mixed bin.


  ## Frequently Asked Questions


  ### What should I keep in a junk drawer?


  Keep only items you use daily or weekly, plus things you would need in a
  pinch, like a flashlight, spare batteries, or a good pen. Everything else
  belongs somewhere more specific.


  ### How long does it take to organize a junk drawer?


  With the timeline above, about 30 minutes. The time goes up if you stop to
  sort sentimental items or wait on a repair. Set a timer and make decisions
  quickly.


  ### How do I organize a junk drawer without buying anything?


  Use boxes, egg cartons, and jar lids as containers. Empty the drawer, sort
  into Keep, Relocate, and Toss, then group the keepers by how you use them.


  ### Is a junk drawer a bad idea?


  Not necessarily. A junk drawer becomes a problem when it has no zones and no
  rule. With a few containers and a clear keep test, it can work as a handy
  catch-all.


  ## Final Takeaway


  To organize a junk drawer in 30 minutes, empty it, sort into keep, relocate,
  and toss piles, measure the space, contain your keepers, and reload by
  frequency. Then use the daily-weekly-or-pinch rule and a five-minute monthly
  check to keep it tidy. For more quick home fixes like this one, browse the
