  If you want to know how to build a landing page without writing code, the
  short answer is this: choose a no-code builder, start from a template, keep
  one goal and one call to action, add a short form, connect it to your email
  tool, and test everything before you publish. This guide walks through each
  step in plain language, with a checklist at the end.


  ## What a no-code landing page is


  A landing page is a single web page built to get one specific action, such as
  an email signup or a purchase. A no-code builder is a drag-and-drop editor
  that lets you place headings, images, buttons, and forms without touching
  HTML, CSS, or JavaScript.


  You do not need a developer, but you still need to make a few decisions well.
  The tool handles the code. You handle the message.


  ## Step 1: Pick one goal


  Every landing page needs one job. Common goals include an email signup, a demo
  request, a purchase, or a webinar registration. Pick one and write it as a
  single sentence, such as: Visitors download our free packing checklist.


  If you cannot finish that sentence, the page is not ready to build.


  For example, a freelance photographer might want consultation bookings, while
  a small bakery might want holiday box pre-orders. Each goal changes the
  layout, the form, and the button text.


  ## Step 2: Choose a builder with clear criteria

  publishing. Instead of picking the most popular name, compare them with the

  ## Step 3: Build the layout in five blocks


  A good landing page usually follows a simple order. You do not need every
  block, but keep the sequence.


  ### 1. The hero


  Put a clear headline, one supporting sentence, and your call-to-action button
  above the fold. The headline should state the benefit, not the feature. Get a
  printable packing checklist for any trip beats Our checklist every time.


  ### 2. The problem and the solution


  Use two or three short sentences to name the problem and show how your offer
  solves it. Keep paragraphs to two or three lines, because visitors skim.


  ### 3. Benefits as bullets


  Use three to five bullets, and make each one describe an outcome. Avoid vague
  claims such as the best solution ever.


  ### 4. Proof


  Add a testimonial with the person's name and role, or a short result you can
  source. If you do not have proof yet, leave the block out instead of inventing
  quotes.


  ### 5. The final call to action


  Repeat the button at the bottom using the same wording as the hero.
  Consistency reduces hesitation.


  ## Use one call to action, not several


  Every extra link gives visitors another option, which can pull attention away
  from your goal. Unbounce data, as reported by
  [ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/marketing/landing-page-conversion-rates),
  found that pages with one link averaged a 13.5% conversion rate, compared with
  11.9% for pages with two to four links and 10.5% for pages with five or more.


  Those are averages across many pages, not a promise. The direction is still
  clear: remove the site navigation, avoid footer links that lead elsewhere, and
  do not place a second button that competes with the first.

  ## Add a basic form and connect it


  The form is where the goal gets completed, so keep it short. A December 2024
  study by NP Digital of 404 landing pages, published on [Neil Patel's
  site](https://neilpatel.com/marketing-stats/conversion-rate-by-form-fields/),
  found that one-field forms converted at 18.2%, two-field forms at 13.0%,
  three-field forms at 11.5%, and four-field forms at 9.9%. Ask only for what
  you need to follow up. For many signups, an email address is enough.


  Follow these steps:


  1. Add a form block with only the fields you need, such as first name and
  email.

  2. Write a button label that describes the outcome, such as Send me the
  checklist, instead of a generic Submit.

  3. Add a consent checkbox and a link to your privacy policy. Ask a legal
  advisor which consent language your audience needs, especially if you collect
  emails from people in several countries.

  4. Set a thank-you message or a redirect to a confirmation page so visitors
  know the submission worked.

  5. Connect the form to your email tool through the builder's native
  integration. If there is no native option, an automation tool can route
  submissions. Our guide to [automating work with Zapier without
  code](https://www.3minread.com/build-ai-agents-without-code-the-ultimate-guide-to-automating-work-with-zapier)
  walks through that kind of setup.


  Then submit a test entry and confirm it appears in your email list or CRM with
  the right fields and the right tag. Do this before you share the link.

  ## Pre-publish checklist


  Run this list before you share the page with anyone:


  - [ ] The headline and button match the one goal

  - [ ] Only one primary call to action is visible

  - [ ] The form works on a phone and on a laptop

  - [ ] A test submission arrives in your email tool with every field filled
  correctly

  - [ ] The thank-you message or redirect appears after submitting

  - [ ] The consent checkbox and privacy policy link are in place

  - [ ] Every image is compressed and has descriptive alt text

  - [ ] Every link opens a working page

  - [ ] No placeholder text remains, such as lorem ipsum or insert text here

  - [ ] Analytics or conversion tracking is installed and records a test visit

  - [ ] The page has been read once for spelling and grammar


  ## Check speed before launch


  Google measures page experience with Core Web Vitals. According to [Google's
  web.dev
  documentation](https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds),
  a good experience means Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) of 2.5 seconds or less,
  Interaction to Next Paint (INP) of 200 milliseconds or less, and Cumulative
  Layout Shift (CLS) of 0.1 or less, measured at the 75th percentile of page
  visits.


  In plain terms, LCP tracks how fast the main content appears, INP tracks how
  quickly the page responds to taps and clicks, and CLS tracks whether elements
  jump around while the page loads.


  Oversized images are one of the easiest fixes. In the same ZoomInfo summary of
  Unbounce data, pages without oversized images averaged 11.4% conversion,
  versus 9.8% for pages with them. Export hero images at the size they will
  display, not the original camera file.


  ## Measure the first version


  A landing page is a starting point. [Unbounce's
  analysis](https://unbounce.com/average-conversion-rates-landing-pages/) of 464
  million visits to 41,000 landing pages (Q4 2024 data) put the median
  conversion rate across industries at 6.6%. Use that as a loose reference, not
  a target, because industry, traffic source, and offer change results a lot.
  Compare your page against its own numbers over time.


  To test a second version without code, check your builder's settings for split
  testing first. If it is not available, publish a second page with one
  difference, such as the headline, send each page equal traffic, and wait until
  you have enough visits to see a clear gap before you choose a winner.

  ## Our verdict


  If you are new to this, start with a template-based builder that includes a
  form tool and a native connection to your email platform. Build the simplest
  version first, with one goal, one button, and a form of three fields or fewer.
  Then improve it with real data.


  At 3minread, we would rather publish a plain page that works than a complex
  page that loses people halfway down.


  ## Frequently asked questions


  ### Can I build a landing page for free?


  Many no-code builders offer a free plan or a free trial, but the limits
  differ. Free plans often restrict custom domains, the number of pages, or
  features such as analytics and integrations. Check the plan details on each
  vendor's pricing page before you start.


  ### Do I need a domain or hosting?


  Most builders host the page for you, so you usually do not need separate
  hosting. A custom domain, such as yourbrand.com, typically requires a domain
  purchase and a connection in the builder's settings. Some free plans show the
  builder's own web address instead.


  ### What is the best no-code landing page builder?


  There is no single best option. The right choice depends on your goal, your
  budget, and the email or CRM tool you already use. Apply the criteria in step
  two to shortlist two or three builders, then test each one with a free trial.


  ### Can AI build a landing page without code?


  Many builders now include AI tools that draft headlines, copy, or full
  layouts. They save time on first drafts, but you still need to check facts,
  adjust the offer to your real product, and run the checklist before
  publishing.


  ### How do I A/B test a landing page without code?


  Look for split testing in your builder's settings first. If it is not
  available, create two versions of the page with one difference, such as the
  headline, and send them equal traffic. Wait until you have enough visits to
  see a clear gap before you pick a winner.


  ## Conclusion


  Learning how to build a landing page without writing code comes down to a few
  decisions: one goal, one clear call to action, a short form, a working
  connection to your email tool, and a checklist before you publish. Start
  simple, measure the results, and improve one element at a time. The builder
  handles the code, and your job is the message.
