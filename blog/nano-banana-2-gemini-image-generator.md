  Gemini, switch to the image tool, describe your scene with concrete details,
  and refine the result in the same chat. Below, we walk through each step,
  share prompt formulas you can copy, and cover what to do when Gemini gets a
  detail wrong.

  ## What “Nano Banana” means in Gemini


  “Nano Banana” started as a nickname for a Google image model. Google later
  adopted the name as the brand for its Gemini image models, so you will see it
  attached to several releases. For more on the launch and what it means for
  creators, read our earlier piece: [Google’s Nano Banana Is Changing AI Image
  Generation Forever—and It’s Free to
  Try](https://www.3minread.com/google’s-nano-banana-is-changing-ai-image-generation-forever—and-it’s-free-to-try).


  Google introduced Nano Banana 2 in February 2026. Google positions it as a
  model that combines Pro-level image generation and editing with Flash-level
  speed, according to [Google Cloud’s enterprise
  announcement](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/bringing-nano-banana-2-to-enterprise).
  You can read the [Google launch
  post](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/ai/nano-banana-2/) for
  the full overview.

  ## How to use the Nano Banana 2 Gemini image generator, step by step


  ### Step 1: Open Gemini and sign in


  Go to Gemini on the web or open the Gemini app on your phone, then sign in
  with your Google account.


  ### Step 2: Find the image tool


  On desktop, open the sidebar from the menu at the top left, then choose
  Images. On mobile, look for the image creation option in the current app,
  since menus can differ by version and device. The [Gemini Apps Help page for
  desktop](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/14286560?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop)
  walks through the process in more detail.


  ### Step 3: Write a specific prompt


  Vague prompts produce generic images. Compare two versions of the same idea:


  - Basic: Create an image of a cat napping.

  - Specific: Generate an image of a ginger tabby napping in a sunbeam on a
  rustic wooden windowsill.


  The second version gives the model a subject, a setting, and light. Use this
  formula:

  Put it together in one line: “Generate an image of a ginger tabby cat napping
  on a rustic wooden windowsill, soft morning sunbeam, close-up with shallow
  depth of field, warm realistic photograph, no text.”


  ### Step 4: Review the result before you reuse it


  Read the image the way an editor would. Check the composition, the main
  subject, and any text, hands, logos, or numbers. AI models still get small
  details wrong, so a polished image is not automatically accurate.


  ### Step 5: Refine in the same chat


  You do not need to start over. Tell Gemini what to keep and what to change:
  “Keep the same composition, but make the lighting warmer and add a ceramic mug
  on the sill.” Naming what should stay the same keeps the image from drifting
  into something new.


  ### Step 6: Download and verify


  Download the file you plan to use and keep the original. If the image makes a
  claim, such as a product feature or a price, check that claim against a
  reliable source before you publish.


  ## How to add text inside an image


  If you want words in the picture, put them in quotation marks inside the
  prompt and say where they should go. For example: Add the text “Open Late
  Tonight” in bold white letters across the top. Keep text short. Long sentences
  and multiple lines are where errors usually appear, so ask for one line at a
  time and check the spelling in the result.

  ## Check provenance with SynthID


  Images made with Gemini include an invisible SynthID watermark, and some
  outputs also carry a visible mark. Keep the original file. If you publish an
  image, label it as AI-generated when readers would reasonably expect that
  context.


  ## Frequently asked questions


  ### Is Nano Banana 2 free in Gemini?


  You can start with a free account, but image generations are limited. Paid
  plans get wider access. Exact limits can change, so check the usage details in
  the app.


  ### Is Nano Banana 2.1 available in the Gemini app?


  Decrypt reports that the 2.1 rollout includes the Gemini app. Availability can
  still depend on your plan, region, and device. If you do not see the newer
  model, the same steps apply to whichever image model your app offers.


  ### Why does Gemini return text instead of an image?


  Usually the prompt reads like a question or a conversation. Start with a clear
  instruction such as “Generate an image of...” and describe what should appear
  in the frame.


  ### Which aspect ratio should I use?


  Put the ratio in your prompt, for example “vertical 9:16 for a phone
  wallpaper” or “wide 16:9 banner.” The model works from your description, so
  crop the final file if you need an exact size.


  ## Takeaway


  - Using the Nano Banana 2 Gemini image generator follows a simple loop:
  prompt, review, refine.

  - Specific prompts beat short ones. Include subject, scene, light, style, and
  constraints.

  - Put any text you want in quotation marks, and check details before you
  publish.

  - Confirm the current model name and plan access in the app or the API docs.
