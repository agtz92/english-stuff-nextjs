  Is Laphroaig 10 peaty? Yes, strongly so. The malt is usually quoted at about
  40 to 45 parts per million (PPM) of phenols, and in the glass that shows up as
  peat smoke, sea salt, and iodine. The number tells you how much peat went into
  the barley. The flavor tells you how it feels. Here is both, plus a clear way
  to decide whether this bottle suits you.


  ## Is Laphroaig 10 peaty? The PPM numbers


  Peat strength is measured as phenol parts per million, or PPM. The figure
  describes the malted barley before distillation, not the finished whisky, so
  it is a guide rather than a direct reading of your glass. Higher PPM generally
  means more peat smoke went into the malt.


  For Laphroaig 10, the figures in circulation are close but not identical:


  - About 40 PPM, the figure in [Whiskipedia's phenol
  guide](https://whiskipedia.com/fundamentals/phenols/) and in several
  comparison lists.

  - About 45 PPM, a figure [The Whiskey
  Wash](https://thewhiskeywash.com/whiskey-articles/laphroaig-vs-ardbeg-where-to-start-if-youre-new-to-peated-whisky/)
  attributes to Beam Suntory, which owns the distillery.


  Why do they differ? Sources quote a spec, a batch, or a rounded figure, and
  they do not always say which. Both numbers place the malt in the same
  strong-peat zone, so read them as a range rather than a precise specification.

  ## Is Laphroaig 10 heavily peated?


  It depends on the scale you use, and this is where most articles get muddled.
  Whiskipedia sorts whisky into three bands: lightly peated at 10 PPM or less,
  medium peated at around 25 PPM, and heavily peated above 50 PPM. By that
  scale, a malt at 40 to 45 PPM sits between medium and heavy, below the heavy
  line.


  Yet many reviewers and shops call Laphroaig 10 heavily peated, and most
  tasters find the smoke heavy in the glass. Our read: by the malt scale it is
  high-medium, but by taste it is heavy. For a newcomer, the flavor matters more
  than the label.


  ## What the peat tastes like


  Reviewers agree on the core profile. Me, My Wife & Whisky describes a nose of
  sea salt, charcoal, and iodine-rich peat. The Whiskey Shelf goes further and
  describes chemical, industrial notes such as burnt rubber or gasoline.
  Opinions split here, which is useful: the same bottle reads as medicinal and
  comforting to some drinkers and as harsh to others.


  Use this checklist on your first pour:


  - Nose: peat smoke, sea air, iodine, and wet ash.

  - Palate: smoke arrives first, followed by salt and a dry, medicinal backbone.

  - Finish: long, smoky, and drying.

  ## Laphroaig 10 vs Ardbeg 10


  Ardbeg 10 is the usual comparison.
  [LegacyDram](https://legacydram.com/blog/ardbeg-10-vs-laphroaig-10-purifier-mickey-heads-bessie-williamson/)
  puts Ardbeg 10 at roughly 50 to 55 ppm of phenols, higher than the 40 ppm
  figure it gives for Laphroaig 10. Peat level does not tell the whole story,
  though. The Liquor Barn describes Ardbeg 10 as darker and tarrier, with more
  citrus-tinged peat, while Laphroaig 10 leans medicinal and iodine-forward.

  ## Which bottle should you buy?


  The standard 10-year-old is bottled at 40% or 43% ABV depending on market,
  according to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laphroaig_distillery).
  [Batch 17 Cask
  Strength](https://www.laphroaig.com/whisky-stories/10-year-old-cask-strength-batch-17)
  is bottled at 58.3% ABV, according to the distillery. The range also includes
  other 10-year releases, such as a Sherry Oak version, so check the label
  before comparing reviews, because bottlings differ.


  Our verdict:


  - Buy the standard 10 if you want to learn the peat and see whether you like
  it.

  - Buy cask strength if you already enjoy big, bold whisky and do not mind
  adding water.

  - Skip it, or start with a lighter style, if smoke and iodine are a turnoff.


  ## A practical path for peat newcomers


  1. Start with the standard 10, neat, in a small pour.

  2. Add a few drops of water and taste again. Water often softens the heat and
  opens up aromas, though that part is a matter of taste.

  3. Once you know the house style, try a lighter Islay to measure the
  difference.

  4. Move to Batch 17 only after the standard feels comfortable, and dilute it
  to taste.

  ## Frequently asked questions


  ### How does Laphroaig 10 compare with other Islay whiskies?


  Its malt figure of about 40 to 45 PPM places it among the strongly peated
  Islay malts. Ardbeg 10 is generally listed higher, so Laphroaig 10 is a strong
  peat but not the heaviest on the shelf.


  ### Is Laphroaig 10 smoky or peaty?


  Both words describe the same thing here. The smoke comes from peat, so expect
  strong, medicinal smoke with iodine and sea salt rather than a light, woody
  smoke.


  ### Is Laphroaig 10 good for beginners?


  It can be, but it is a demanding first Islay, and some tasters find it
  overwhelming at first. Start with a small pour, add water, and consider a
  milder peated whisky first if big smoke puts you off.


  ### What proof is Laphroaig 10?


  The standard bottling is 40% or 43% ABV depending on market. Batch 17 Cask
  Strength is 58.3% ABV.


  ### How old is Laphroaig 10?


  It is a 10-year-old single malt, matured in ex-bourbon barrels, according to
  the distillery's own page.


  ## Takeaway


  Is Laphroaig 10 peaty? Yes. The malt is usually quoted at about 40 to 45 PPM,
  which is strong peat by most measures, even though it falls below the heavy
  line on one popular scale. If you like iodine and sea salt, it is a rewarding
  pick. Start with the standard bottling, add a little water, and step up to
  cask strength only when you are ready.
