  Looking for how to clean mechanical keyboard switches without a full teardown?
  Start with a dust-first routine. Unplug the board, blow out loose debris, and
  wipe the switch stems with a lightly dampened swab. This surface clean takes
  about 15 minutes and handles most of the grime that builds up between keys.
  Save full disassembly for sticky or unresponsive keys.


  ## Decide Between a Surface Clean and a Deep Clean


  Pick the level that matches the problem:


  - **Surface clean (about 15 minutes):** Dust, crumbs, and light grime. You
  don't remove keycaps.

  - **Deep clean (about 1 to 2 hours):** Sticky keys, double-typed letters, or
  grime under the keycaps. You remove keycaps for this.


  Before you open anything, check your warranty. Some guides warn that opening
  switches may void it, so read the manufacturer's terms first. Then check
  whether your board is hot-swap, meaning switches pull out with a puller, or
  soldered, meaning they are fixed to the circuit board. Soldered boards should
  stick to surface cleaning and a keycap-level deep clean. If you are still
  sorting out how switch types differ, our guide to [Mechanical Keyboard Switch
  Types: Linear, Tactile,
  Clicky](https://www.3minread.com/mechanical-keyboard-switch-types) covers the
  basics.

  ## How to Clean Mechanical Keyboard Switches: Surface Checklist


  Work through these steps in order and check off each one as you go.


  - [ ] **Unplug and photograph the layout (2 minutes).** Keycap positions are
  easy to forget, and a photo saves time later.

  - [ ] **Turn the board upside down and tap it over a trash can (1 minute).**
  Loose crumbs should fall out without force.

  - [ ] **Blow out debris with air (3 minutes).** Hold the nozzle at a shallow
  angle so the air doesn't loosen the stabilizers.

  - [ ] **Brush between keys, front to back (2 minutes).** Skip the stabilizer
  gaps, where debris can slip down toward the top plate and PCB.

  - [ ] **Dampen a swab with isopropyl alcohol (1 minute).** Squeeze out the
  excess. It should be damp, not dripping.

  - [ ] **Wipe around each stem and the gaps between keys (5 minutes).** Use
  short, light strokes. Switch to a fresh swab once it looks dirty.

  - [ ] **Air-dry for 10 minutes, then check for fibers (1 minute).** Remove any
  loose threads before you plug the board back in.


  ### Choosing Your Alcohol Strength


  Strength advice varies. [Keebs For
  All](https://keebsforall.com/blogs/mechanical-keyboards-101/how-to-remove-old-lube-from-switches)
  recommends 90% or higher, while [MKB
  Guide](https://mkbguide.com/blog/mechanical-keyboard-cleaning-maintenance)
  says 70% to 90% is the sweet spot. Pick a strength within the range these
  guides give, keep the liquid on the swab, and let the board dry completely.

  ## Deep Clean Steps for Sticky Keys


  Use this method only when a surface clean doesn't fix sticky or double-typed
  keys.


  1. **Pull the keycaps (5 minutes).** Use a keycap puller and lift each cap
  evenly to avoid bending stems.

  2. **Soak the keycaps in warm soapy water (about 15 minutes).** Rinse them
  with clean water and set them on a towel to dry.

  3. **Remove switches only on hot-swap boards (10 to 20 minutes).** Use a
  switch puller and pull straight up. On soldered boards, leave the switches in
  place.

  4. **Clean the top plate and switch housings with damp swabs (10 minutes).**
  Work gently and don't force anything.

  5. **Dry everything before reassembly.** Keycaps usually dry within a few
  hours, but give the whole board at least 48 hours before powering it on.
  Seventy-two hours is better when you can wait.

  ## Stabilizers: Clean Them Without Forcing Them


  Stabilizers support long keys like space, shift, and enter. They are easy to
  disturb, so keep the work gentle. Blow out dust with air at a shallow angle,
  and don't pry at the housings or push liquid into them. Keebs For All also
  suggests a monthly blast of compressed air on stabilizer housings, a
  low-effort habit for a board you use every day. Lubing stabilizers is a
  separate project and isn't part of this routine.


  ## Mistakes That Can Damage Your Switches


  - **Dripping liquid onto the board.** Keep the alcohol on the swab, not on the
  switches or the PCB.

  - **Using a household vacuum.**
  [MechKeyReview](https://mechkeyreview.com/blog/tutorials/how-to-clean-a-mechanical-keyboard)
  warns that vacuums can pull off keycaps and damage stems. Use a blower
  instead.

  - **Reaching for WD-40 or harsh sprays.** Manufacturer advice cited by HHKB
  says WD-40 isn't safe on some keyboard plastics.

  - **Forcing switches out of a soldered board.** Pulling can bend pins and
  damage the board.

  - **Rushing the dry.** Powering on a damp board can cause shorts.

  - **Working near heat or open flame.** Isopropyl alcohol is flammable, so keep
  it away from ignition sources.


  ## How Often Should You Clean?


  Guidance varies.
  [HP](https://www.hp.com/us-en/tech-takes/accessories/maintenance/how-to-clean-and-maintain-mechanical-keyboard.html)
  suggests a dust-off every couple of weeks and a thorough clean every 6 to 12
  months, while
  [MelGeek](https://www.melgeek.com/blogs/melgeek-lab/how-to-clean-mechanical-keyboard-switches)
  recommends a deeper clean every 3 to 6 months. Our take: for a daily-use
  board, a quick blow-out every two weeks and a surface clean once a month keeps
  dust down. Save the deep clean for once or twice a year, or sooner when keys
  start acting up. This is our opinion rather than a fixed rule, so adjust for
  your desk and environment.

  ## Frequently Asked Questions


  ### Can I clean switches without removing them?


  Yes, for dust and light grime. Blow out debris and wipe around the stems with
  a damp swab. Remove keycaps only when keys stick or grime sits under the caps.


  ### Can I use water on my mechanical keyboard?


  Don't pour or drip water onto the board. A damp swab is enough for surface
  grime, and everything should dry fully before you power it on.


  ### How do I know if a switch is dead or just dirty?


  If the key works normally after cleaning and drying, it was dirty. If it still
  fails or double-types after a deep clean, the switch may be worn or damaged
  and may need replacing.


  ### Can I use isopropyl alcohol under the keycaps?


  Yes, sparingly. Use a damp swab, keep liquid away from the circuit board, and
  let the board dry completely.


  ### Will cleaning void my warranty?


  It can. Some guides warn that opening switches may void warranties, so read
  your manufacturer's terms before a deep clean. Surface cleaning carries less
  risk.


  ## The Bottom Line


  Clean the surface often with air and damp swabs, keep the stabilizers gentle,
