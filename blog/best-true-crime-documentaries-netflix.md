  The best true crime documentaries on Netflix are the ones you can actually
  finish, so this guide sorts our picks by runtime and subject. Each title comes
  with a content warning and one reason to press play, so you can choose
  tonight's watch based on how heavy you want the material to be.


  If you have one evening, start with the short films below. If you want
  something to binge over a weekend, the docuseries section has the longer
  options. Ratings and runtimes are listed as they appear on [Netflix's True
  Crime hub](https://www.netflix.com/tudum/features/true-crime), but they can
  change and may differ by region, so confirm them on each title page before you
  press play.


  ## Best true crime documentaries on Netflix under 90 minutes


  These are the quickest picks and work well for a single sitting.


  ### What Jennifer Did (1h 27m, TV-14, 2024)


  **Content warning:** Family violence and a planned attack on parents.


  **Why watch it:** It is the shortest pick here, and its tight structure keeps
  the timeline easy to follow in one sitting.


  ### Unknown Number: The High School Catfish (1h 34m, TV-MA, 2025)


  **Content warning:** Online deception, emotional manipulation, and harassment
  involving teenagers.


  **Why watch it:** It is a modern case about how an online identity can hide
  real intentions. It suits viewers who enjoy internet sleuthing.

  ## Two-hour picks for a relaxed evening


  ### Athlete A (1h 44m, PG-13, 2020)


  **Content warning:** Sexual abuse of young athletes, survivor testimony, and
  institutional failures.


  **Why watch it:** It focuses on accountability, following the reporters and
  survivors who pushed a powerful organization to face what happened. It spends
  its time on the investigation rather than on the abuser.


  ### The Menendez Brothers (about 2 hours, TV-MA, 2024)


  **Content warning:** Graphic descriptions of the killings of the brothers'
  parents and allegations of childhood sexual abuse.


  **Why watch it:** The courtroom drama and the long public debate over the case
  make this a strong pick if you like legal storytelling. Runtime varies
  slightly between listings, so check the title page.

  ## Docuseries for a weekend binge


  ### The Staircase (13 episodes)


  **Content warning:** A death at a family home, courtroom footage, and a long
  legal process.


  **Why watch it:** Its unusual access across years lets you watch a case unfold
  slowly. It suits anyone who likes following twists over time.


  ### Night Stalker (4 episodes)


  **Content warning:** Murders and sexual violence, including home invasions,
  with some crime-scene material.


  **Why watch it:** It follows the hunt for one of the most notorious serial
  killers in American history, and it reads like a police procedural.


  ### The Idaho Murders (3 episodes)


  **Content warning:** The 2022 killings of four University of Idaho students,
  with graphic crime-scene imagery and deep grief.


  **Why watch it:** It is a recent, contained case told in three parts, so it is
  easy to finish in a couple of evenings.


  ### Bad Influence: The Dark Side of Kidfluencing (3 episodes, TV-MA, 2025)


  **Content warning:** Child exploitation, online harassment, and the pressure
  of family vlogging.


  **Why watch it:** It looks at the business of children's online content and
  what can go wrong. It is useful viewing for parents and anyone who follows
  creators.


  ### Abducted in Plain Sight (2017)


  **Content warning:** Childhood abduction, sexual abuse, and abuse by someone
  the family trusted.


  **Why watch it:** Directed by Skye Borgman, it explores how people around a
  family missed the warning signs. It is a serious, carefully framed story.


  ### The Trials of Gabriel Fernandez


  **Content warning:** Child abuse and the death of a child.


  **Why watch it:** It concentrates on the case and the legal process rather
  than shock value. It is a good match if you enjoy crime stories but dislike
  sensationalism and exploitation.

  ## How to read ratings and content warnings


  Netflix shows a rating on each title. According to the [FCC's V-Chip
  guide](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/v-chip-putting-restrictions-what-your-children-watch),
  TV-MA means a show is designed for adults and may be unsuitable for children
  under 17. TV-14 means it contains material many parents would find unsuitable
  for children under 14. PG-13 comes from the film rating system, so treat it as
  a rough guide for a documentary.


  Look for content descriptors too. V signals violence, S signals sexual
  content, L signals coarse language, and D signals suggestive dialogue. Our
  warnings above are written to help you decide quickly, but the descriptors on
  each title page tell you more.

  ## Frequently asked questions


  ### What is the best true crime documentary on Netflix right now?


  It depends on how much time you have. For a short watch, start with What
  Jennifer Did. For a longer, slow-burn case, The Staircase is a strong choice.


  ### Are there true crime documentaries short enough for one night?


  Yes. The under-90-minute section above is built for a single sitting, with
  What Jennifer Did and Unknown Number at the top.


  ### What does TV-MA mean?


  TV-MA signals that a title is designed for adults and may be unsuitable for
  children under 17. It usually means graphic material, strong language, or
  intense themes.


  ### Which true crime documentaries are based on solved cases?


  Netflix's Solved list is the best place to start, since it groups titles with
  a resolved outcome.


  ### Are these titles available everywhere?


  Not necessarily. Netflix catalogs vary by country, so search for each title on
  your local Netflix before you plan your watch.


  ## The takeaway


  Choose a short pick if you have one evening, or a docuseries if you want a
  weekend. Read the content warning before you press play, and let the rating
  guide your choice.
