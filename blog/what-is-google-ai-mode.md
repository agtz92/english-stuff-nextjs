  So, what is Google AI Mode? It's Google's conversational search experience.
  Instead of showing one ranked list of links, it breaks your question into
  smaller searches, runs them at the same time, and writes a single answer with
  sources you can open. You can keep asking follow-up questions in the same
  thread, which makes it feel more like a conversation than a search box.


  That is the short version. Below, we explain how it works, compare it with
  regular Search and AI Overviews, and give you four test queries to run
  yourself, so you can see the difference with your own questions.


  ## How Query Fan-Out Works


  Google's name for the core technique is query fan-out. In its I/O 2025 update
  on AI Mode, Google said the feature “uses our query fan-out technique,
  breaking down your question into subtopics and issuing a multitude of queries
  simultaneously on your behalf.”


  In plain terms, a broad question gets split into parts. For example, a
  question about planning a budget weekend trip might be broken into transport,
  free attractions, and places to eat. The system runs those searches together
  and combines the results into one reply.


  That is what makes AI Mode different from a normal search. Three things stand
  out:


  - It answers in prose first, with links underneath.

  - It keeps the thread, so follow-ups build on the previous answer.

  - It can ask a clarifying question when your request is vague.


  Google's help pages say you can ask follow-up questions on a topic, and
  [Google's Search Labs help page for AI
  Mode](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/16296315?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop)
  describes the feature in more detail.


  ### What Is Google AI Mode, According to Google


  Google calls AI Mode its most powerful AI search experience. The [official
  AI-powered responses help
  page](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/16011537?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop)
  explains how to get those answers in Search. The marketing language is less
  useful than the mechanics: one question goes in, many searches run in the
  background, and one answer comes out.

  ## Four Test Queries You Can Run Yourself


  The fastest way to understand AI Mode is to run the same question in both
  places and compare. Use a private window so your search history doesn't shape
  the results, and keep the wording identical.


  ### 1. Whisky: Where to Start with Smoky Scotch


  Try: “I like smoky flavors. Should I start with an Islay scotch or a bourbon?”


  What to compare: Does the answer explain the taste differences in plain
  language? Does it name a few starter bottles and say why? Regular Search will
  usually send you to tasting notes and retailer pages, so you do more of the
  comparing yourself.


  ### 2. Google Sheets: A Budget That Updates Itself


  Try: “How do I make a monthly budget in Google Sheets where the totals update
  automatically?”


  What to compare: Are the steps in the right order? Does the answer name the
  formulas it uses? Test the formulas on a copy of your sheet. If you want a
  full walkthrough of a Sheets project, our guide on [building a calendar in
  Google
  Sheets](https://www.3minread.com/how-to-build-a-fully-functional-calendar-in-google-sheets-like-a-pro)
  shows the same kind of step-by-step approach.


  ### 3. Mechanical Keyboards: What Matters for Long Typing Sessions


  Try: “What should I look for in a mechanical keyboard for coding all day, and
  is hot-swappable worth paying extra for?”


  What to compare: Does the answer give you criteria, such as switch feel,
  layout, and noise, or just a list of brands? Good answers say what changes
  your typing experience and what is only a preference.


  ### 4. Mac for Programming: How Much Memory You Need


  Try: “How much RAM do I need on a Mac to run Docker and a code editor at the
  same time?”


  What to compare: Does the answer separate minimums from comfortable setups?
  Does it link to official Apple or Docker documentation? Multi-part questions
  like this are where AI Mode tends to save the most clicks, but you still need
  to confirm the numbers.


  ## Where to Find AI Mode on Desktop and Mobile


  Access has changed several times since AI Mode was introduced in March 2025,
  and it can depend on your region, account, and rollout status. If something
  below doesn't match what you see, check Google's help page.


  **On desktop:**


  1. Go to google.com/ai, or open Google Search and look for the AI Mode option.

  2. Type your question as you would say it out loud. Full sentences work well.

  3. Use the follow-up box to refine the answer. Start a new search when you
  want a fresh topic.


  **On mobile:**


  1. Open the Google app.

  2. Look for AI Mode near the search bar or in the Search tab.

  3. Tap the sources under the answer to check them before you act on them.


  Button labels change, so if the name is different on your phone, look for the
  AI-powered option.

  ## When to Use AI Mode and When to Use Regular Search


  Use AI Mode when:


  - Your question has several parts, such as a comparison or a plan.

  - You want Google to narrow a broad topic before you dig in.

  - You're exploring and don't yet know the right keywords.


  Use regular Search when:


  - You need a specific website or official page, such as “Lagavulin 16 official
  site.”

  - You need an exact figure, date, or product spec from one source.

  - You want to compare several independent sites yourself.


  The pattern is simple. AI Mode is good at shaping a question. Regular results
  are good at finding the original source.


  ## How Accurate Are AI Mode Answers?


  Google itself warns that, as with any early AI product, AI Mode doesn't always
  get things right. Treat every answer as a draft until you check it. A simple
  routine helps:


  - Open at least two of the cited sources, and check their dates.

  - Confirm numbers, prices, and dates against the original page.

  - Run any code or spreadsheet formula on a copy of your data first.

  - For purchases, check the retailer page for current price and specs.

  - If a follow-up changes the answer, trust the version that cites primary
  sources.


  ## How Big Is AI Mode?


  Usage has grown quickly. Alphabet's remarks on October 29, 2025, as summarized
  by [Mission Growth's AI Mode statistics
  roundup](https://missiongrowth.io/blog/ai-mode-statistics), put AI Mode at
  more than 75 million daily active users. Query length has grown too. [PPC Land
  reports](https://ppc.land/google-ai-mode-hits-1-billion-users-how-search-changed-in-one-year/)
  that the average AI Mode query is now about three times as long as a
  traditional search query, and that Google has said AI Mode queries have more
  than doubled every quarter since launch.


  These are snapshots, and usage figures change fast. Our view is that the
  longer, more conversational queries are the real shift. The strongest
  counterargument is that answers delivered on the results page can mean fewer
  clicks for publishers, and a summary can flatten nuance. Both points are worth
  keeping in mind when you decide how much to trust a single answer.

  ## Frequently Asked Questions


  ### What is Google AI Mode, and is it the same as Gemini?


  No. AI Mode is a search experience inside Google Search. It is built on Gemini
  models, but it is designed around search results, links, and sources. Gemini
  is a general AI assistant.


  ### What is Google AI Mode's difference from AI Overviews?


  AI Overviews is a summary shown above regular results. AI Mode is a full
  conversational answer with its own follow-up thread. Many quick questions
  start as AI Overviews and move to AI Mode when they get more complicated.


  ### What is Google AI Mode used for?


  People use it for multi-part questions, comparisons, and planning. The
  sections above show the situations where it tends to help most, and where
  regular Search still works better.


  ### Does AI Mode replace regular Google Search?


  Not for every task. For specific websites, official pages, and exact facts,
  classic results are often faster. AI Mode works best as a second tool for
  multi-part questions.


  ### Can AI Mode help with tasks like checking availability?


  Google's help pages describe some task features, such as checking local
  availability and pricing and monitoring a topic for new updates. What works
  depends on your region and settings, so check the help page before you rely on
  it.


  ### How do I check whether an AI Mode answer is right?


  Open the cited sources, confirm the numbers and dates, and test any code or
  formulas on a copy of your data. The checklist above covers the routine.


  ## Takeaway


  So what is Google AI Mode in one line? It is a conversational layer over
  Google Search. It splits your question into several searches, combines the
  results, and lets you follow up. Use it for multi-part questions and
  comparisons, and use regular Search when you need a specific source or exact
  fact. Always check the answer before you act on it.
