  Looking for the best co-op games for two players? For most couches, the answer
  starts with It Takes Two, a split-screen adventure built around a pair of
  characters who have to work together. Below are our picks for two players on
  one couch, with a short take on each and a simple way to choose.


  ## How we scored each pick


  - **Learning curve:** Can a player who has never held a controller join within
  a few minutes?

  - **Session length:** Does the game suit a 30-minute night, or does it want a
  full evening?

  - **Screen setup:** Is it split-screen, where each player gets their own view,
  or shared-screen, where you both watch one camera?


  Shared screen works well for puzzles and party games, because you both see the
  same thing at the same time. Split-screen gives each player room to explore.
  Neither is better in every case, so we use these three checks for every pick
  to keep the list consistent at 3minread.


  ## Do you need two copies?


  Usually not. For couch play, one owned copy is typically enough, and the
  second player joins on the same console or PC. The exception to watch for is
  EA's Friend's Pass for It Takes Two. It lets a friend join the full game for
  free in online co-op if the other player owns it, according to [EA's official
  FAQ](https://www.ea.com/games/it-takes-two/it-takes-two/faq). Because the pass
  is primarily an online feature, the couch setup is simpler than many readers
  expect. Steam's [store page for It Takes
  Two](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1426210/It_Takes_Two/) also says an EA
  account is no longer required for local play.


  The cost math is simple. The list price cited by
  [Silvergames](https://www.silvergames.com/en/best-co-op-games) for It Takes
  Two is $39.99. At that price, two copies would cost about $80, while one copy
  on the couch costs about $40. Prices change, so check the store on the day you
  buy.

  ## Our picks for two players


  ### 1. It Takes Two


  Hazelight Studios developed It Takes Two, Electronic Arts published it, and it
  launched on March 25, 2021. It won Game of the Year at the 2021 Game Awards.
  The story follows a couple whose relationship is falling apart, and each
  player controls a different character with mechanics that only work when you
  cooperate.


  - **Learning curve:** Low to moderate. New mechanics arrive often, so expect a
  few minutes of settling in.

  - **Session length:** Chapter-based, so it is easy to stop at a natural break.

  - **Screen setup:** Split-screen local co-op.


  **Verdict:** The safest first pick if you want one game you will both finish.


  ### 2. Portal 2


  [Co-op.gg](https://www.co-op.gg/splitscreen) lists Portal 2 as split-screen
  through the whole two-player campaign on PC and Switch. Each player moves
  through puzzle chambers, and the portals one player creates often open the way
  for the other. The best sessions sound like a conversation.


  - **Learning curve:** Moderate. Puzzles get harder, and you will need to think
  out loud.

  - **Session length:** Flexible. One chamber fits a short evening, and a longer
  run can stretch much further.

  - **Screen setup:** Split-screen.


  **Verdict:** Pick it if you both enjoy puzzles more than action.


  ### 3. A Way Out


  A Way Out is designed exclusively for two players, so nobody waits for a third
  person to join. It is a prison-break story that asks both players to plan and
  act together.


  - **Learning curve:** Low. The controls are simple, which helps when
  experience levels differ.

  - **Session length:** Story-driven, so plan a longer evening.

  - **Screen setup:** Varies by platform, so check the store page before you
  buy.


  **Verdict:** A good choice for a couple who wants a story to share from start
  to finish.


  ### 4. Cuphead


  Cuphead is a shared-screen run-and-gun game with a steep difficulty curve. Two
  players can team up, but a seasoned player paired with a newcomer can quickly
  end up frustrated, so match the pair before you start.


  - **Learning curve:** High. Boss patterns take practice.

  - **Session length:** Short bursts. A single boss fight is brief, but failing
  the same one again and again can feel long.

  - **Screen setup:** Shared screen.


  **Verdict:** Best for two players at similar skill levels who want a
  challenge. Skip it if one player is a beginner.


  ### 5. Pico Park


  Pico Park is a shared-screen puzzle game where players have to cooperate to
  get everyone to the exit. It supports two to eight players on current
  versions, so two players are a comfortable fit.


  - **Learning curve:** Very low. Anyone can join in minutes.

  - **Session length:** Short rounds that suit a 30-minute night.

  - **Screen setup:** Shared screen.


  **Verdict:** The easiest pick for mixed-experience couples and non-gamers.


  ### 6. Stardew Valley


  Stardew Valley supports up to four players on one screen since its 1.5 update,
  so two players can farm the same valley together. Each in-game day is short,
  so you can stop after a few days without losing your place.


  - **Learning curve:** Low to start, with plenty of depth later.

  - **Session length:** Flexible.

  - **Screen setup:** Shared screen.


  **Verdict:** A calm, low-stress pick for couples who like building things
  together. If you want more gentle options, our [guide to cozy games for
  beginners](https://www.3minread.com/best-cozy-games-beginners) covers calm
  picks.


  ### 7. Fireboy and Watergirl


  Fireboy and Watergirl is a free two-player game that Silvergames lists as
  playable on one keyboard. One player controls Fireboy and the other controls
  Watergirl, and each character can safely cross only the hazards that match
  them. Teamwork is the whole puzzle.


  - **Learning curve:** Low.

  - **Session length:** Short levels that work well for a quick break.

  - **Screen setup:** One keyboard, two sets of controls.


  **Verdict:** The best no-cost option, and it needs no extra purchase.

  ## How to choose between them


  - **One game for a full evening:** It Takes Two.

  - **Puzzles and conversation:** Portal 2.

  - **Mixed skill levels:** Pico Park or Stardew Valley.

  - **Zero budget:** Fireboy and Watergirl.

  - **A short weeknight:** Pico Park or a board game like Sky Team.


  ## Frequently asked questions


  ### What is the best co-op game for two players?


  It Takes Two is the most consistent answer. It is designed around duo play,
  and one copy supports split-screen couch co-op. If you want something shorter
  or simpler, Pico Park and Fireboy and Watergirl are better fits.


  ### Do both players need to buy the game?


  Usually not. For couch play, one owned copy is typically enough. Check the
  store page for each title, because rules differ by platform and some games
  have their own pass or sharing terms.


  ### Are there free two-player co-op games?


  Yes. Fireboy and Watergirl is a free two-player game that Silvergames lists as
  playable on one keyboard.


  ### Which co-op game works best for a couple who are not gamers?


  Pico Park is the easiest start because the rules take minutes to learn and
  rounds are short. It Takes Two is a strong second choice if you want a story
  to share, since it is built around a couple's relationship. If you want more
  calm, beginner-friendly options, see our [cozy games for beginners
  guide](https://www.3minread.com/best-cozy-games-beginners).


  ### Can I play split-screen on PC?


  Yes, many titles support it. Co-op.gg maintains a filterable list of
  split-screen games by platform and player count, which is useful for checking
  a specific title before you buy.


  ## The takeaway


  Start with It Takes Two if you want one reliable pick. Choose Portal 2 for
  puzzles, Pico Park for mixed skill levels, and Fireboy and Watergirl for a
  free night. Match the game to your pair rather than just picking the top of
  the list, and you will spend more of the evening playing and less of it
  waiting.
