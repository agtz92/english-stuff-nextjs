  To freeze rows in Google Sheets on desktop, select the row you want to keep
  visible, click View, choose Freeze, and pick the number of rows. On a phone,
  touch and hold the row number and tap Freeze. Frozen rows stay on screen while
  you scroll, so your headers don't disappear when a sheet runs to hundreds of
  lines. If you're wondering how far a sheet can grow, see the [10 million cell
  limit in Google
  Sheets](https://www.3minread.com/google-sheets-10-million-cell-limit-powerful-potential-or-hidden-trap-for-your-data).


  This guide covers the fastest desktop methods for how to freeze rows in Google
  Sheets, how to freeze rows and columns together, the mobile steps for Android
  and iPhone or iPad, a practical order-tracking example, and the fixes for a
  frozen layout that looks wrong.


  ## How to freeze the top row on desktop


  Freezing your header row is the most common job, and Google Sheets gives you
  three ways to do it. For the full desktop steps, see Google's [Docs Editors
  Help page for
  desktop](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/9060449?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop).


  ### Option 1: Use the View menu


  1. Click the number 1 on the left side of the grid to select the top row.

  2. Click View in the top menu bar.

  3. Hover over Freeze and choose 1 row.


  If your headers take up two rows, choose 2 rows instead. Those rows will stay
  put as you scroll down.


  ### Option 2: Drag the gray bar


  Look for the thin gray divider at the top-left of the grid, just under the
  column letters and to the right of the row numbers. Drag it downward until it
  sits below the last row you want frozen. Release it, and the rows above stay
  visible.


  ### Option 3: Right-click a row number


  Right-click the row number you want to freeze. In the menu, choose the freeze
  option that matches how many rows you need. This is the quickest route if your
  mouse is already on the sheet.

  ## How to freeze rows and columns together


  Many sheets need both: a header row at the top and an ID or name column on the
  left. To set that up, freeze the first row using View > Freeze > 1 row. Then
  open the same menu, choose Freeze again, and select 1 column.


  Keep in mind that frozen sections always start from the top-left corner. You
  can't freeze a single row in the middle of the sheet. If you select row 5 and
  freeze it, rows 1 through 5 all stay visible. Columns work the same way. If
  you need a row in the middle to stay visible, move it to the top first.


  ## How to freeze rows on mobile


  Mobile is where layouts vary most, so learning how to freeze rows in Google
  Sheets on a phone takes a little more checking. Menu options differ between
  the Android app and the iPhone and iPad app, and they can change between app
  versions, so check what your screen shows.


  ### Android


  1. Open the file in the Google Sheets app.

  2. Touch and hold the row number you want to freeze.

  3. Tap Freeze in the menu.


  Some Android versions also let you pick a number of rows. According to
  [Android
  Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/google-sheets-freeze-unfreeze-rows-columns/),
  you scroll to the bottom of the menu and tap the up arrow to choose how many
  rows or columns to freeze. For Google's own Android instructions, see the
  [Docs Editors Help page for
  Android](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/9060449?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid).


  ### iPhone and iPad


  1. Open the file in the Google Sheets app.

  2. Tap the row number or column letter.

  3. Tap the right arrow in the menu, then tap Freeze.


  Google documents the same steps in its [iPhone and iPad help
  page](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/9060449?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DiOS).


  ### If you can't freeze multiple rows on your phone


  Some guides report that the mobile app is more limited and can't freeze
  several rows or columns. If you need more than one frozen row on your phone,
  set up the layout on a computer first. The app respects freeze settings you
  apply on desktop, so the layout carries over when you open the file on your
  phone.

  ## How to unfreeze rows


  To remove frozen rows on desktop, click View, then Freeze, then choose No
  rows. To remove frozen columns, choose No columns. On the Android app, touch
  and hold the row number and tap Unfreeze. On iPhone and iPad, tap the row
  number or column letter, tap the right arrow, and tap Unfreeze.

  ## Freezing doesn't lock cells


  Freezing only keeps rows and columns visible while you scroll. It doesn't stop
  anyone from typing in them. If you need to limit edits, use Data > Protect
  sheets and ranges to set who can edit a specific range. Changing freeze
  settings requires Editor access to the sheet, so viewers can't adjust the
  layout.


  If you're coming from Excel, the same feature is called Freeze Panes, under
  View. In Sheets, the menu item is simply Freeze.


  ## Frequently asked questions


  ### Can I freeze rows in the Google Sheets app on my phone?


  Yes. The Android and iPhone or iPad apps both offer freeze options from the
  row number or column letter menu. Options can vary by app version, and
  multiple-row freezing can be limited on mobile, so desktop is the more
  reliable choice for complex layouts.


  ### Can I freeze an entire sheet at once?


  No. Google Sheets doesn't let you freeze a whole sheet in one step. You can
  freeze rows at the top and columns on the left, which covers most needs.


  ### Why did my frozen rows move after I added a column?


  Frozen rows don't reposition automatically when the layout changes. Remove the
  freeze with View > Freeze > No rows, then freeze the correct rows again.


  ### How do I freeze a row that isn't at the top?


  You can't freeze a single row in the middle without freezing every row above
  it. Move the row to the top of the sheet, or freeze through it.


  ## Takeaway


  Knowing how to freeze rows in Google Sheets takes seconds: use View > Freeze
  on desktop, or touch and hold the row number on mobile. Freeze your header row
  first, add the key column if you need it, and remember that freezing only
  keeps things visible. For editing controls, use Protect sheets and ranges.
